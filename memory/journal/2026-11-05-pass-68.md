# Pass 68 — the market becomes a register, not a reading list

**Date:** 2026-11-05 · **Phase:** P7 · **Kind:** research and memory only

## Why

The project had strong competitor prose but no governed market record. The
parity matrix carried five competitor columns from 2026-10-31; the research
folder held four long pages and an "Others" page; the pass-67 README pointed at
a gap register that did not exist. There was no way to answer three questions
from machine truth:

1. Which bots does a mid-size server actually run, and what is free?
2. Which missing capability does each one prove is worth building?
3. What is the smallest next step, and which claim supports it?

Pass 68 fixes that. The research now has a schema, cross-checks and a generated
human page, and every bot row traces to one expiring claim.

## What changed

- **`memory/machine/competitors.json`** is the ledger: 18 bots, 6 generalists
  (MEE6, Dyno, Carl-bot, ProBot, Arcane, Lurkr), 9 specialists (Ticket Tool,
  Helper.gg, Wick, Double Counter, Statbot, Jockie, Apollo, GiveawayBot,
  UnbelievaBoat) and 3 AI-native entrants (PeakBot, YourBot, Wallu). Each row
  records the official site, one source page, the free and paid boundary, the
  strongest capability, the weakness, the gap note, its gap ids, its research
  claim and a confidence.
- **`memory/machine/gaps.json`** is the build order: 18 gaps, each with a
  priority, phase, size, status, feature ids, target competitors, claim ids and
  a smallest next step. Seven are `open`; eleven are `partial`, meaning the
  foundation ships and one visible capability is missing.
- **`memory/machine/research.json`** gained R-021 to R-038, one claim per bot,
  every one with a source, retrieval date, confidence and expiry. Prices are
  list prices read on 2026-11-05, not promotional rates; Wick and Wallu are
  `medium` because their pricing was read through a third-party page or a
  partly rendered vendor page.
- **`memory/tools/memory-check.mjs`** now checks the ledger and the register,
  not only their shape: ids are unique, each kind exists, each bot's claim
  exists and has topic `competitor`, every gap id and feature id resolves,
  priorities are unique, every gap names a real competitor and claim, and a
  competitor that names a gap is named back by that gap. GiveawayBot is allowed
  an empty gap list because parity is the honest answer; the checker requires
  that its note says so.
- **`memory/tools/render-memory.mjs`** generates `Competitor-Index.md` and
  `Gap-Register.md` from those machine files. The vault index, the map of
  content and `Competitive-2026.md` link to them.
- No command, reply, interaction, dependency or test changed.

## Verification

- `pnpm memory:render` wrote the two new pages and refreshed the five existing
  generated pages.
- `pnpm memory:check`: **4,550/4,550** checks passed, including 18 competitors,
  18 gaps, 38 research claims, 139 local markdown links and 448 vault wikilinks.
- `pnpm check`: green; 631 tests in 92 files, 20/100 roots, 35/35 claims.
- Every price names the exact page and the date, and the claim expires on its
  own date.

## What we tried and rejected

- **Add 18 competitor columns to `features.json`.** Rejected: 134 rows times 18
  vendors is unreadable, most cells would be empty, and the existing five-column
  matrix is already a summary rather than a source. A separate ledger with one
  claim per bot is governable.
- **Treat "free" as a binary.** Rejected: the useful boundary is the cap. "Free
  leveling" can mean 3 role rewards, no voice XP and a 100-user leaderboard;
  recording that is what makes the gap honest.
- **Copy Double Counter's network detection.** Rejected: it depends on shared
  IP, device and behavioural data across servers. Keystone will use local
  signals and an optional provider port, with a privacy note, not a central
  surveillance graph.
- **Build YourBot's plugin marketplace.** Rejected: unreviewed third-party code
  is a supply-chain and trust problem, and the roadmap already refuses it. The
  useful part is an AI planner with a preview and an approval gate.
- **Force every competitor to have a non-empty gap list.** Rejected:
  GiveawayBot's requirements and reroll are already shipped. Inventing a gap to
  make a table look busy would be marketing, not research.
- **Make one claim per sentence.** Rejected: 18 claims, one per vendor's pricing
  page, is enough to audit the ledger. Splitting every free-tier cap into its
  own claim would make the research file unreadable without making it truer.
